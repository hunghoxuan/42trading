- Swing trading: Hold 1–5 days. D/4h → 1h → 15m -> 5m
- Day trading: Open and close same day. 4h/1h → 15m → 5m -> 1m

Step 1: Open the HTF chart first
Identify the macro, dominant trend and Overvall market direction. Mark the most recent major swing high/low, trend phase. Draw the key horizontal S/R levels.  
Question: What is the dominant trend ? Are we in bull or bear structure ? Where are the major decision zones ?
Output: directional bias (bullish / bearish / neutral). Never enter trade against this. Key price levels to watch. Sets the target and invalidation.

Step 2: Move to the Setup TF — look for trade opportunity, find the setup
Look for a recognisable pattern (flag, triangle, double bottom, etc.) that is forming in the direction of the HTF bias. Confirm that the Setup TF structure supports the trade. If Setup TF is trending against the daily, skip the trade entirely.
Question: is valid setup forming right now ? 
Output: recognized patterns or signals you will trade

Step 3:  Drop to LTF (Entry TF) — define entry zone, local S/R, momentum confirmation.
Find the exact zone where price is likely to react. Mark your entry price, stop loss (behind the nearest structure), and TP1 / TP2 based on daily S/R. Calculate R:R — if below 1:2, do not take the trade.
Question: where exactly do i get in and place my trade setup ?
Output: entry price zone · stop loss · TP1 · TP2 · R:R ratio

Step 4: Final confirm with Trigger TF
Check last candles a candlestick signals (engulfing, hammer, doji) or a mini-breakout on the Trigger TF that confirms momentum is turning in your direction. This is your "green light." Without this confirmation, you are guessing at timing.
Question: Is this the right time to trade ?
Output: Trigger signals confirmation. Go now/wait signal.

Pre-entry checklist — all must pass before making trade plan
Checklist 1: HTF trend aligned
Daily or Weekly trend is in the same direction as your trade. Trading a long in a HTF downtrend is fighting the current — skip it.
Checklist 2: 4H structure valid
4H is forming higher highs / higher lows (for longs) or lower lows / lower highs (for shorts). No structure break against you in the last 3 candles.
Checklist 3: Clear pattern or setup visible
You can name the setup: "Bull flag on 4H," "Double bottom at 1D support." If you cannot name it, there is no setup.
Checklist 4: Entry at confluence zone
At least 2 factors meet at your entry: Fib level + S/R, or EMA + pattern neckline, or order block + FVG. Single-factor entries have much lower win rates.
Checklist 5: Volume confirms the move
If volume bars are visible: breakout candles should have above-average volume. Pullback candles should have below-average volume. Reverse is a warning sign.
Checklist 6: R:R minimum 1:2
Distance to TP1 must be at least 2× the distance to your stop loss. If the nearest S/R level is too close to TP, the trade is not worth taking at this price.
Checklist 7: No major news within 2 hours
Check the economic calendar. CPI, NFP, central bank decisions will invalidate all technical setups. Wait until after the event to enter or reduce size significantly.
Checklist 8: Do not enter if: conflicting signals on 3+ TFs
If HTF says bullish, SetupTF says bearish, and EntryTF is choppy — there is no trade. Conflicting structure means institutional consensus does not exist yet. Wait.
Checklist 9: Do not enter if: price is mid-range
Entries should be at the edge of a range or structure, not in the middle. Mid-range entries have equal probability of going either way — that is not a trade, it is a coin flip.

Rules handle TF conflicts — the most important skill

Rule 1: HTF always wins
If Daily is bearish, do not take a long on 4H no matter how perfect it looks. The higher timeframe defines the environment. Lower TF patterns inside a HTF downtrend are counter-trend trades with a lower win rate and should be avoided or traded with 50% size at most.
Rule: never fight the daily trend with a full-size position

Rule 2: 2 out of 3 alignment = acceptable
If 3 TFs are checked and 2 agree with the trade direction, the setup is valid but reduce position size by 25–50%. Example: Daily bullish + 4H neutral + 1H bullish = valid long at 50% size.
Rule: 2/3 aligned = trade at half size

Rule 3: 3-way conflict = no trade, wait for resolution
Daily bullish + 4H bearish + 1H bullish = choppy, no consensus. This is the market deciding direction. Wait for the 4H to align before entering. These periods often precede the sharpest moves — patience here is the edge.
Rule: 3-way conflict = stand aside, set alerts

Rule 4: LTF conflict = timing issue, not thesis issue
Daily + 4H bullish but 15m showing a pullback = this is not a conflict. The 15m pullback IS the entry opportunity. This is exactly what you want to buy. The thesis is intact; you are just waiting for the right candle.
Rule: LTF pullback against HTF trend = entry opportunity

Rule 5: After a structure break on HTF — re-evaluate everything
If a 4H or Daily support breaks cleanly (candle closes below, not just a wick), all open bullish theses are immediately invalidated. Do not average down. Re-analyse from the top of the TF stack before considering any new entries.
Rule: HTF structure break = full re-analysis required

Key differences
Chart patterns — what they are
They identify the situation. A H&S pattern tells you a reversal is likely. But it says nothing about exactly when or where to get in. They are the map, not the vehicle.
Entry models — what they do
They define the precise moment you click buy or sell. Retest entry, Fib level, order block — these tell you the exact candle and price to act on.
Using them alone
Pattern alone = no clear entry price, wide stop, poor R:R. Entry model alone = no directional context, random signals with no narrative behind them.
Using them together
Pattern gives context + target. Entry model gives timing + stop. Together they form a complete trade: direction, entry, stop, target — all defined before you enter.
